Washer Won't Spin or Drains Poorly: What Homeowners Can Check

Few laundry problems are as frustrating as opening the washer to find a heavy, soaking-wet load sitting in gray water. When a washing machine will not spin or drains poorly, the cause is often something simple — an unbalanced load or a clogged filter. Sometimes it is a worn part that needs a technician. These checks will help you sort out which situation you are in.

Common Symptoms

  • The cycle ends with clothes still dripping wet
  • Water remains in the bottom of the tub after the cycle
  • The washer drains very slowly, or not at all
  • The machine shakes, walks across the floor, or bangs during spin
  • The washer stops mid-cycle and will not restart
  • A musty smell comes from the tub, especially on front-load models

The Most Common Causes

An unbalanced or oversized load. One heavy item — a bath mat, a blanket, a pair of soaked jeans — can throw the load off balance. Modern washers detect this and refuse to spin at full speed to protect themselves.

A lid switch or door latch problem. Top-loaders will not spin if the machine thinks the lid is open; front-loaders will not start or spin if the door latch is not engaging. A worn latch can fail even when the door looks closed.

A clogged drain pump filter. Coins, hairpins, lint, and small clothing items collect in the filter (on most front-loaders) or in the pump itself. A blocked filter is the single most common reason a washer drains poorly.

A kinked or clogged drain hose. A hose that got pinched when the washer was pushed back, or one with buildup inside, slows drainage dramatically.

A worn drive belt or failing drain pump. These are mechanical parts that wear out over years of use. When they fail, the washer needs a repair rather than a reset.

Safe Checks You Can Do First

Unplug the washer before reaching into the tub area, tilting the machine, or touching hoses and filters.

  1. Redistribute the load. Open the washer, spread the clothes evenly around the tub, remove a few items if it was packed tight, and run the spin cycle again. This alone solves many no-spin complaints.
  2. Check the lid or door. Close it firmly and listen for the latch click. If the machine starts when you press down on the lid, the switch may be wearing out.
  3. Inspect the drain hose. Pull the washer out a few inches and look for kinks or crushed sections where the hose runs to the standpipe or laundry sink.
  4. Clean the drain pump filter (front-loaders). Most front-load washers have a small access door near the bottom front. With towels and a shallow pan ready — water will come out — open it, unscrew the filter slowly, clear out debris, and rinse it clean.
  5. Run the drain hose into a bucket test. If you can safely hold the hose over a laundry sink and run a drain cycle, watch the flow. A weak trickle suggests a clog in the hose or pump rather than a control problem.

Do not remove the washer's outer cabinet, test electrical components, or bypass a lid switch. Those are not safe DIY jobs.

When to Call a Professional

Call for service if:

  • The washer still will not spin with a small, balanced load
  • You hear grinding, squealing, or a loud hum during drain or spin
  • The filter and hose are clear but water still sits in the tub
  • The machine leaks while draining
  • The problem keeps coming back after cleaning the filter

A pump that hums but does not move water, or a tub that will not turn at all, usually means a part has failed and further cycles can make the damage worse.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my washer fill and agitate but not spin? Agitation and spinning use different actions from the same motor system. A washer that washes but will not spin often has a lid switch, latch, belt, or control issue — or it is simply protecting itself from an unbalanced load. Try the balanced-load test first.

How often should I clean the drain pump filter? On front-load washers, every few months is a sensible habit, and sooner if loads start draining slowly or smelling musty.

Can a clogged filter really stop the whole cycle? Yes. Many washers will not spin or advance the cycle if the water cannot drain to a safe level first.
