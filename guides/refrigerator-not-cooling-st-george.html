Refrigerator Not Cooling in St. George: Common Causes and First Checks

A refrigerator that stops cooling is never a small problem, and in St. George it can turn urgent fast. When summer temperatures push past 100 degrees, a fridge that is already struggling has to work even harder, and food can spoil quickly. The good news is that some cooling problems have simple causes you can check yourself in a few minutes. Others need a technician. Here is how to tell the difference.

Common Symptoms

Cooling trouble often starts gradually rather than all at once. Watch for:

  • Food in the fresh-food section spoiling faster than usual
  • The refrigerator running almost constantly, or sounding louder than normal
  • The freezer staying cold while the refrigerator section feels warm
  • Ice or frost building up on the back wall of the freezer
  • Puddles of water inside the fridge or underneath it
  • The outside of the cabinet feeling unusually warm between the doors

Any one of these is a sign the refrigerator is not moving cold air the way it should.

The Most Common Causes

Dirty condenser coils. The coils release the heat the refrigerator removes from inside. In dusty desert conditions they collect dust, pet hair, and grit, and a coated coil cannot shed heat efficiently. The compressor works overtime and cooling suffers.

Blocked air vents. Cold air travels from the freezer into the refrigerator section through vents. Food packages stacked against those vents can block the flow, leaving the fridge warm while the freezer stays cold.

Worn door gaskets. The rubber seal around the door keeps cold air in. If it is cracked, loose, or warped, warm kitchen air leaks in all day and the refrigerator can never quite catch up.

Incorrect temperature settings. Settings sometimes get bumped accidentally. The refrigerator section should sit around 37–38°F and the freezer at 0°F.

A failing fan or frost buildup. The evaporator fan circulates cold air, and the defrost system melts frost off the coils. When either one stops working, cooling drops — and these are repair-level problems.

Safe Checks You Can Do First

Always unplug the refrigerator before reaching behind or under it.

  1. Check the temperature settings. Make sure the controls were not changed by accident, and give any adjustment a full 24 hours to stabilize.
  2. Look at the vents. In both the freezer and refrigerator sections, move food away from the vent openings so air can circulate.
  3. Test the door seal. Close the door on a dollar bill. If you can pull it out easily with almost no drag, the gasket is not sealing well at that spot. Repeat around the door.
  4. Clean the condenser coils. With the fridge unplugged, find the coils (behind a rear panel or under the front grille, depending on the model) and vacuum them with a brush attachment. This one step fixes a surprising number of "warm fridge" calls.
  5. Check the space around the fridge. A refrigerator pushed tight against the wall or boxed in by cabinets cannot breathe. Leave a small gap for airflow.

Do not attempt to open sealed refrigeration components, recharge refrigerant, or repair wiring. Those jobs require specialized tools, certification, and training.

When to Call a Professional

Call for service if:

  • The refrigerator is still warm 24 hours after cleaning the coils and checking the basics
  • You hear clicking, buzzing, or the compressor starting and stopping rapidly
  • Frost keeps building up on the freezer's back wall
  • The fridge is cold in some spots and warm in others despite clear vents
  • The unit is completely warm and silent, or warm and running nonstop

Catching a cooling problem early often means the difference between a straightforward repair and a compressor failure that is not worth fixing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my freezer cold but my refrigerator warm? This usually points to an airflow problem: blocked vents, a fan that is not circulating air, or frost choking the evaporator coils. Start with the vent and coil checks above. If airflow looks clear and the fridge stays warm, the fan or defrost system likely needs attention.

How cold should my refrigerator be? Around 37–38°F for the refrigerator section and 0°F for the freezer keeps food safe without overworking the appliance.

Is it worth repairing an older refrigerator that stopped cooling? Often, yes — many cooling problems are caused by a single worn part rather than total failure. A proper diagnostic tells you exactly what failed and what the repair involves, so you can decide with real information instead of guessing.
