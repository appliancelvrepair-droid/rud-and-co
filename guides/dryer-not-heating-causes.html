Dryer Not Heating: 6 Causes From Lint to Heating Element

A dryer that tumbles but produces no heat leaves you with a drum full of damp clothes and a cycle that never seems to end. The drum turning is actually useful information: it tells you the motor and belt are probably fine, and the problem lives in the dryer's heating system or its airflow. Here are the six causes we see most often, starting with the one you can fix yourself.

First, a Quick Symptom Check

Before anything else, note what the dryer is doing:

  • Tumbles normally but the air inside stays cool
  • Gets a little warm but clothes take two or three cycles to dry
  • Heats for the first few minutes, then goes cold
  • Shuts off quickly or runs forever without drying

Each pattern points in a slightly different direction, but the causes below cover most cases.

Cause 1: A Clogged Vent or Lint Buildup

This is the most common cause of poor drying — and the most overlooked. When the vent hose or the outside vent is packed with lint, moist air cannot escape. The dryer overheats internally, and a safety device cuts the heat to prevent damage. The result: long cycles, damp clothes, and eventually a dryer that stops heating altogether. A clogged vent is also a genuine fire risk, which is why this check comes first.

Cause 2: A Blown Thermal Fuse

The thermal fuse is a one-time safety switch that cuts power to the heating element if the dryer gets too hot — usually because of the venting problem above. Once it blows, it does not reset. The dryer will tumble but never heat until the fuse and the underlying airflow problem are both addressed.

Cause 3: A Failed Heating Element (Electric Dryers)

The heating element is a coil of wire that glows hot when current passes through it. After years of heating and cooling cycles, the coil can break. When it does, the dryer runs normally in every other way — it just produces no heat at all.

Cause 4: A Faulty Igniter or Gas Valve (Gas Dryers)

Gas dryers light a burner to make heat. If the igniter wears out, the burner never lights and the drum stays cold. Sometimes the dryer heats at first and then stops, which can point to gas valve coils weakening as they warm up.

Cause 5: A Bad Cycling Thermostat

Thermostats regulate the temperature inside the drum by switching the heat on and off. A thermostat that fails can cut the heat too early or keep it off entirely, leaving clothes lukewarm and damp.

Cause 6: Power Supply Problems

Electric dryers run on 240 volts. The drum motor can turn on half that power, but the heating element needs the full supply. If one side of the circuit trips, the dryer tumbles perfectly and heats not at all — a sneaky problem that starts at the breaker panel, not in the dryer.

Safe Checks You Can Do First

Unplug the dryer (and shut off the gas supply valve on a gas dryer) before doing anything beyond cleaning the lint screen.

  1. Clean the lint screen thoroughly. Wash it with warm water and a brush if fabric-softener residue has filmed it over. Let it dry completely.
  2. Check airflow at the outside vent. With the dryer running, hold your hand at the exterior vent. You should feel a strong, warm stream of air. Weak airflow means a restriction.
  3. Inspect the vent hose. Look behind the dryer for a crushed, kinked, or sagging hose. Replace damaged flexible hose rather than trying to patch it.
  4. Check the breaker panel (electric dryers). Look for a partially tripped double breaker and reset it fully — off, then on.
  5. Don't overload. An overpacked drum traps moisture and mimics a heating problem.

Do not open the dryer cabinet, test heating elements or fuses with a meter, or work on gas lines or igniters. Those are technician jobs with real safety stakes.

When to Call a Professional

If airflow is strong, the breaker is fine, and the dryer still does not heat, the problem is internal — a fuse, element, igniter, or thermostat. Call for service, and stop running the dryer in the meantime: a dryer that overheats or runs with a burning smell should stay off until it is checked.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my dryer dry fine on some settings but not others? Timed and sensor cycles use the heat system differently, so heat problems sometimes show up on one setting first. It is still worth checking venting, since restricted airflow affects every setting eventually.

Can a clogged vent damage other parts? Yes. Restricted airflow makes the dryer run hotter than it should, which is the leading reason thermal fuses blow and elements fail early. Fixing the vent protects the replacement parts.

Is a no-heat dryer worth repairing? Usually. Fuses, elements, igniters, and thermostats are standard repair parts, and a dryer that tumbles well has its expensive motor still working.
