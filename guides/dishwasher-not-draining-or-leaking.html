Dishwasher Not Draining or Leaking: Causes and Safe Checks

Standing water in the bottom of the dishwasher at the end of a cycle — or worse, a puddle spreading across the kitchen floor — ranks among the most unwelcome appliance surprises. The encouraging part: drainage and leaking problems often trace back to a handful of common causes, and several of them are safe to check yourself. Here is how to work through it.

Common Symptoms

  • A pool of water remains in the bottom of the tub after the cycle ends
  • Water drains slowly, or the dishwasher sounds like it is straining to drain
  • A puddle appears in front of the dishwasher during or after a cycle
  • Dishes come out with gritty residue or a cloudy film
  • The dishwasher stops mid-cycle and will not continue
  • A sour or musty smell comes from the tub between loads

The Most Common Causes

A clogged filter. Every dishwasher has a filter at the bottom of the tub that catches food debris. When it is coated, water cannot reach the drain pump properly and collects in the bottom instead. This is the most common cause of poor draining, and the easiest to fix.

A blocked drain hose. The hose that carries water from the dishwasher to the sink drain or garbage disposal can kink, or collect grease and debris over time. A partially blocked hose drains slowly; a fully blocked one does not drain at all.

A disposal connection issue. If your home has a garbage disposal, the dishwasher usually drains through it. A clogged disposal, or a drain inlet that was never opened during a disposal replacement, can back water straight into the dishwasher.

A clogged air gap or sink drain. Many kitchens have a small cylinder on the sink deck — the air gap — that prevents backflow. When it clogs with debris, drainage suffers. A slow sink drain causes similar trouble.

Leak sources: door seal, spray arms, and suds. Leaks at the front often come from a worn door gasket, a cracked spray arm jetting water at the door, or suds from the wrong detergent — dish soap creates foam that forces its way past seals.

A failing drain pump. If the water paths are clear and the machine hums, clicks, or stays silent instead of draining, the pump itself may have worn out. That is a repair, not a cleaning job.

Safe Checks You Can Do First

Dishwashers combine water and electricity, so be careful: switch the dishwasher off at the breaker before reaching under the machine or handling connections, and mop up standing water around the base before you begin.

  1. Clean the filter. Twist out the cylindrical filter at the bottom of the tub, rinse it under the tap, scrub gently with a soft brush, and reinstall it. While it is out, look for broken glass or debris in the sump area — using a flashlight and gloves, not bare hands.
  2. Run the garbage disposal. If you have one, run it with cold water for 30 seconds to clear the shared drain path, then try the dishwasher's drain or cancel function again.
  3. Check the air gap. Pop the cap off the sink-deck cylinder and remove any visible debris inside.
  4. Inspect the drain hose for kinks. Look under the sink where the hose runs. A hose crushed by stored bottles and cleaners is a common find.
  5. Confirm the detergent. Use only detergent made for dishwashers, and skip the extra rinse-aid experiments if you are seeing foam and leaks together.
  6. Watch where a leak starts. If the puddle appears at the front corners, suspect the door seal. Clean the gasket with a damp cloth — grit along the seal lets water escape.

Do not pull the dishwasher out of its cabinet, disconnect plumbing or wiring, or remove the door panels. Those steps risk water damage and electrical hazards.

When to Call a Professional

Call for service if the tub still holds water after the filter, hose, and disposal checks; if the machine leaks from underneath rather than at the door; or if you hear the drain pump strain or grind. Acting early matters with water — a small leak ignored for months can quietly damage the flooring beneath the machine.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a little water in the bottom of the dishwasher normal? A small amount of water around the filter in the sump is normal — it keeps seals from drying out. A pool covering the bottom of the tub after a completed cycle is not.

Why does my dishwasher drain into the sink? Hearing water run into the sink drain is normal; that is the shared drain path working. Water backing up into the sink itself means the drain beyond the connection is clogged.

Can I keep using a dishwasher that leaks a little? It is better not to. Even a slow leak can damage flooring and cabinets under and around the machine. Run the safe checks, and if the leak continues, schedule service before the next load.
